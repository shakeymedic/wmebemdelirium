Types of Delirium: Don't Miss the Quiet One

Hypoactive Delirium (77%)

Presents with drowsiness, apathy, withdrawal, and reduced speech. Often misdiagnosed as depression or fatigue.

⚠️ Significantly under-recognised and associated with worse outcomes.

Hyperactive Delirium (23%)

Presents with agitation, restlessness, aggressive behaviour, and wandering. More readily recognised by staff.

Screening: Use The 4AT

Recommended by NICE & SIGN

A score of ≥3 indicates probable delirium with ~88% sensitivity and specificity.

Go to The4AT.com
1. Alertness: Is the patient drowsy, agitated or stuporous?
2. AMT4: Age, Date of Birth, Place (hospital), Current Year.
3. Attention: Months of the year backwards.
4. Acute Change: Evidence of acute change or fluctuation in mental status.

Find the Cause: Common Precipitants

Drugs

  • Anticholinergics
  • Opioids
  • Benzodiazepines
  • Steroids
  • Polypharmacy

Infection

  • UTI (common)
  • Pneumonia
  • Sepsis
  • Any febrile illness

Metabolic

  • Hypo/hyperglycaemia
  • Electrolytes (Na+, Ca++)
  • Hypoxia
  • Renal / Hepatic failure
  • Dehydration

Other Acute Illness

  • Acute MI
  • Stroke / ICH
  • Uncontrolled Pain
  • Urinary retention
  • Constipation
  • Withdrawal (EtOH, Benzo)

Management: Non-Pharmacological First

First-Line Interventions

  • Environment: Adequate lighting, reduce noise, provide clocks/calendars.
  • Support: Frequent reorientation, involve family, ensure patient has glasses & hearing aids.
  • Physiology: Maintain hydration & nutrition, optimise pain control, manage bowels/bladder.
  • Medication Review: Stop offending agents. Deprescribe.

Pharmacology (With Caution)

Only if non-pharma fails AND patient is distressed or a risk to self/others.

  • Haloperidol: 0.5-1mg PO/IM (Max 2mg/24h in frail).
  • Cautions: Monitor ECG for QTc prolongation. Avoid in Parkinson's & Lewy Body Dementia.
  • Lorazepam: For alcohol/benzo withdrawal delirium.

Discharge & Follow-Up

Document Clearly

Document the diagnosis of delirium in notes and discharge summaries.

Educate Patient & Family

Inform them that delirium can take weeks to months to fully resolve.

Arrange Follow-Up

Arrange community cognitive assessment. Avoid diagnosing dementia within 6 months of a delirium episode.
